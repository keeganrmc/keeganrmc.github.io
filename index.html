PROJECT 01•Pet Hardware • Home Living & User Experience•2025

CleanStep Odor-Free Cat Care & Litter System

A furniture-grade cat care architecture featuring a non-porous stainless steel basin, paw-cleaning catch ramp, and integrated tool caddy.

CleanStep addresses the most dreaded chore of cat ownership: daily litter box maintenance. Plastic litter boxes quickly become scratched by cat claws, trapping bacteria and ammonia odors that cannot be washed away. CleanStep pairs a hygienic, non-porous stainless steel basin with an integrated paw-cleaning exit step and modular supply storage, elevating pet care into a clean, quiet household fixture that belongs in modern living spaces.

View Full Resolution
The Real-World Problem

Why Current Solutions Fall Short

Cat owners frequently postpone scooping and washing litter boxes because conventional plastic pans are messy, absorb foul ammonia odors, and allow cats to scatter litter across living room floors.

Design Objective

A seamless, furniture-grade enclosure combining a sanitary 304 stainless steel basin that never absorbs odor, an exit ramp that traps stray granules, and a magnetic tool dock for immediate, clean daily maintenance.

Design Takeaway:Low-incline perforated entry step allows gentle cat entry while catching tracked granules.

ALINE Ultralight Seating Architecture

An ultralight seating system converting standard trekking poles into an ergonomic 115° lean-back trail chair.

ALINE eliminates the tradeoff distance backpackers face between packing heavy 2 lb camp chairs or suffering on cold, damp logs after grueling elevation gains. By leveraging the axial stiffness of trekking poles already in hand, ALINE creates a stable triangulated A-frame combined with a high-tenacity monofilament backrest sling and an insulated waterproof seat pad.

View Full Resolution
The Real-World Problem

Why Current Solutions Fall Short

Backpackers hike 15+ miles a day carrying 30+ lb loads. At camp, sitting on damp granite or fallen logs causes acute lumbar fatigue. However, existing camp chairs carry a 1.5–2 lb weight penalty and consume critical internal pack volume.

Design Objective

Convert the hiker’s existing walking tools into primary load-bearing furniture. The entire system packs down to the size of a water bottle and weighs under 290 grams while delivering true ergonomic back support.

TACT Ergonomic Input Tool

An ergonomic CAD mouse shaped through 14 hand-carved foam mockups to eliminate repetitive wrist strain.

TACT is a wireless input tool designed for designers and modelers who spend full workdays in CAD software. By rotating the hand from traditional flat desk pronation into an anatomically neutral 52-degree handshake angle, forearm tendon tension is significantly reduced while maintaining fingertip dexterity for micro-precision surface modeling.

The Real-World Problem

Why Current Solutions Fall Short

Conventional ergonomic mice force a frustrating compromise: extreme vertical mice feel clumsy for fine micro-movements in CAD, while traditional flat mice pinch the carpal tunnel during prolonged modeling sessions.

Design Objective

A sculpted 52° handshake volume with a flared thumb shelf that supports the resting hand without sacrificing cursor precision.

Methodology • How I Work

From Workshop Mockups to Production CAD

Good industrial design is not about dressing up an engineering chassis after the fact. It is a rigorous process of hands-on prototyping, testing with human hands, and collaborating closely with toolmakers.

01

Observation & Ergonomic Research

Observing how people naturally hold, carry, and interact with objects in daily life. Mapping hand grasp positions, wrist angles, and friction points before committing to a form.

02

Sketching & Form Exploration

Rapid ideation using felt-tip pen, marker, and digital sketching. Exploring 30 to 50 silhouette directions to test proportions, gesture, and character without software friction.

03

1:1 Scale Foam Mockups

Carving high-density blue and pink foam by hand in the workshop. A product on a computer monitor always distorts physical scale—holding a 1:1 block in the hand reveals ergonomic truth immediately.

04

CAD Surfacing & Tooling Details

Refining complex Class-A surfaces in SolidWorks and Rhino. Resolving parting line placement, draft angles, sink mark prevention, snap fits, and mechanical fastening.

05

CMF & Material Execution

Selecting genuine materials that patina gracefully: bead-blasted aluminum, warm turned timber, tactile PBT, and food-grade silicones. Specifying exact mold textures and finishes.

Hi, I’m Keegan.

Download Resume (PDF)

I am a senior Industrial Design student at Kendall College of Art and Design (KCAD) with a background in engineering from Michigan State University. My core design philosophy is simple:

“I design products that solve real-world problems and address real user needs.”

What I enjoy most about industrial design is figuring out how a product can work better for the person actually using it. Whether it is redesigning daily cat litter maintenance to eliminate bacteria-trapping plastic, converting hiking poles into an ultralight backcountry chair, experimenting with touchless air-percussion interactions, or testing ergonomic task seating at Haworth, I believe the best ideas come from listening to users, understanding physical constraints, and building iterative mockups in the workshop.

My process is equal parts hands-on and digital. I spend just as much time cutting corrugated cardboard, sculpting high-density foam, sketching thumbnail gestures on paper, and testing 3D prints as I do developing production CAD in SolidWorks, Creo, and Rhino.

User-Centered Problem SolvingEvery project starts with human friction. If a design doesn’t genuinely improve the user’s daily experience, no amount of styling can save it.
